PROJECT-SPECIFIC · lede + project facts

In late 2023, the operator of an established gold mine in the West Wits Basin engaged Geosynthetics Africa to lift the lining capacity of an existing tailings storage facility by approximately one-third — without interrupting the ongoing depositional cycle on the active facility. Eleven months later, we handed over 320,000 m² of HDPE composite lining, fully tested, electrically leak-surveyed, and accompanied by a regulator-ready CQA pack.

PROJECT-SPECIFIC · the challenge framing
01The Challenge

Lift four of a live TSF — no shutdown allowed.

The operator's Lift 4 expansion is on the critical path for the next phase of life-of-mine. Tailings deposition cannot pause; dust suppression, water management and decant operations all depend on the facility staying live throughout construction.

The lining works had to:

  • Be sequenced around active depositional cycles, with continuous coordination with the mine's environmental team
  • Achieve full compliance with the Global Industry Standard on Tailings Management (GISTM) and the operator's internal Tailings Operating Procedure
  • Survive scrutiny by the Independent Tailings Review Board (ITRB) at three formal hold-points
  • Deliver a leak-tight composite system across a footprint of irregular geometry on a 4.2° upstream slope
  • Be welded and tested at site temperatures swinging from 4 °C (winter overnight) to 32 °C (summer afternoons)
PROJECT-SPECIFIC · the system designed
02The Solution

An eight-layer composite engineered for the site.

Working from the operator's outline tailings management plan, our design team produced a layer-by-layer composite specification, signed off by the lead engineer and reviewed by the ITRB before any material was ordered.

System build-up (top to bottom)

  • Cushion geotextile — Tiltex PP non-woven, 800 g/m², welded panels above the primary liner to protect against deposited tailings impact
  • Primary HDPE — GSE 2.0 mm smooth, tailings-grade resin, single-side textured on slope sections
  • Drainage composite — Flowtex 16 mm cuspated core with bonded geotextile filters — leak-detection layer and pressure relief between primary and secondary
  • Secondary HDPE — GSE 1.5 mm smooth — the redundant hydraulic barrier
  • GCL — Eurobent 5 kg/m² sodium bentonite — secondary low-permeability seal across the full footprint
  • Geogrid reinforcement — Tensar TriAx TX160 in the anchor trench berms only
  • Cushion geotextile — 400 g/m² between GCL and prepared subgrade
  • Compacted subgrade — 600 mm of select earthworks fill, compacted to 95% Mod AASHTO

PROJECT-SPECIFIC · installation depth (the differentiator)
04Installation

11 months. 14 crew. Zero rework.

The installation phase ran from January through November 2024 with three IAGI-certified Master Welders leading two-track wedge welding crews, supported by an extrusion welder for detail work around penetrations, anchor trenches and tie-ins.

Site setup

  • Dedicated material laydown with shaded storage and roll-handling crane
  • Mobile QC laboratory on-site for daily destructive seam coupons (peel/shear)
  • Weather monitoring station — welding suspended below 5 °C ambient or above 70 °C surface
  • Two parallel welding crews running offset by 48 hours, with the trailing crew picking up the previous day's surveyed seams

Sequencing

The works were broken into 12 panel zones, each averaging 26,500 m², each closed out independently with its own seam-test register and CQA sign-off. This let depositional cycles continue on completed zones while construction progressed on adjacent areas.

05QA / QC

No seam left unverified.

Every metre of welded seam was tested twice — non-destructively along its full length, and destructively at the rate of one coupon per 150 m of seam — and the results recorded in the project Seam Test Register before the panel was signed off.

The QA/QC pack handed over

The complete CQA pack runs to 412 pages and is regulator-ready for the operating life of the facility. Filed and indexed for audit access at any time:

PROJECT-SPECIFIC · outcome
07Outcome

Handed over, on time, leak-free.

The completed facility was handed over to the operator in November 2024 with a signed CQA Certificate from the appointed independent CQA Engineer, and accepted at the third and final ITRB review without conditions.

The lining is now in service. Tailings depositional rate matches the original mine plan, and the leak-detection system between the primary and secondary HDPE has registered zero positive readings since commissioning.
