01The TSF Challenge

A TSF must contain. For decades.

Tailings Storage Facilities are among the most consequential containment structures a mining operation builds. They must perform under hydraulic head, dynamic loads, seismic events and chemical attack for the full operating life of the mine and beyond closure — without leakage. The Global Industry Standard on Tailings Management (GISTM) has raised the regulatory and reputational stakes substantially.

Regulatory exposure

GISTM, ICMM Tailings Standard, MASAP, national mining and environmental codes — TSF design and execution is auditable to many overlapping standards. Documentation is not optional.

Leakage liability

Tailings seepage into groundwater is the single largest source of post-closure liability in mining. A correctly designed and certified composite lining contains, monitors and proves performance.

Execution discipline

The lining is a system, not a product. Welding quality, anchor trenching, layer interfaces, leak detection and as-built records all determine performance — and all require certified installers and rigorous QA/QC.

Composite containment in 4 functions

  • Containment. Primary and (where required) secondary HDPE geomembranes form the principal hydraulic barrier — selected for tensile strength, puncture resistance, stress-crack resistance and tailings chemistry compatibility.
  • Protection. Heavyweight cushion geotextiles above the liner protect against impact from deposited tailings, ore fines and equipment. Underliner geotextiles protect against subgrade irregularities.
  • Drainage & leak detection. Drainage composites between the primary and secondary liners provide controlled leak detection, pressure relief and seepage collection.
  • Reinforcement. Geogrids and GCLs supplement the system where bearing capacity, base sealing or sliding stability is critical — particularly on slopes and at anchor trenches.

Why composite, not single-liner?

Modern best practice — and most regulators — require a composite system because no single layer covers all failure modes. The combination of geomembrane (hydraulic barrier), geotextile (protection), drainage composite (monitoring) and GCL (redundant sealing) gives the project both resilience and provability.

Geosynthetics Africa supplies and installs every layer of the composite. The interfaces between layers — where most failures occur — are designed and inspected as a single system rather than as a stack of separately-procured products.

04Our TSF Services
Full lifecycle delivery.
Design through certification.

Every TSF project we deliver moves through the same five service stages. Each stage has defined inputs, a defined output document, and a hold-point before the next stage starts.

Stage 01

Design

Composite specification, drawing review, anchor trench detailing, leak-detection layout, slope stability and seismic checks.

→ Design report
Stage 02

Supply

Stock allocation, mill certificates, batch numbering, transport coordination, on-site delivery scheduled to programme.

→ Delivery dossier
Stage 03

Install

IAGI-certified welders, panel layout, anchor trench excavation, seam welding (hot-wedge / extrusion), penetration detailing.

→ As-built drawings
Stage 04

Test

Vacuum & air-pressure seam testing, destructive shear / peel coupons, electrical leak survey on completed liner.

→ Test pack
Stage 05

Certify

Construction Quality Assurance (CQA) report, traceability matrix, regulator submission pack, handover to operator.

→ CQA certificate
